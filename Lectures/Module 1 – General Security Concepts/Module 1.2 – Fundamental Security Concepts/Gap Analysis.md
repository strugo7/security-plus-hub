# Lesson: Gap Analysis
**Module:** 1.2

## Introduction: Improving Security Posture
There is always room for improvement in the organization's Security Posture. To make the network more secure, we must understand where we aspire to be. The primary tool for accomplishing this task is a Gap Analysis. As its name implies, it is a study that compares where we are today with where we would like to be. In the world of information security, we perform this process continuously to understand exactly what level of security will be required in the future.

## 1. The Nature and Scope of the Process
*Practical Context:* Security managers cannot "guess" what is missing. The gap analysis turns gut feelings into a data-driven work plan.

## 2. Establishing a Baseline
Before starting the gap analysis, it is mandatory that we have a Baseline. The baseline gives us a goal to strive for and a clear idea of where the organizational objectives should be.
*Sources for baselines:* You can choose from a variety of existing baselines, some of which were created for specific organizations:
1. *NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology):* A common document is SP 800-171 Revision 2.
    ◦ *Document title:* "Protecting Controlled Unclassified Information in Nonfederal Systems and Organizations".
2. *ISO/IEC:* A baseline created by the International Organization for Standardization and the International Electrotechnical Commission.
    ◦ *Relevant standard:* ISO/IEC 27001, which deals with information security management systems.
3. *Custom Baseline:* Organizations can create their own standards based on their specific needs.

## 3. Analyzing People and Processes
These baselines often require an in-depth analysis of two main factors: the people in the organization and the processes used for security.
**A. Evaluating People:** You must deeply understand the team's capabilities:
• What is their formal experience in information technology security?
• What kind of Training did they receive?
• Do they have knowledge regarding specific Security Policies and Procedures that can be used in the organization?

**B. Evaluating Systems and Processes:** Even with the right people, you must ensure they operate according to the correct policies.
• The analysis begins by comparing existing IT systems against the formal policies documented in the central security policy documents.
• The goal is to identify weaknesses in the existing systems.
• At this stage, the weaknesses are compared against the most effective processes to compensate for them.

## 4. Breaking it Down: A Practical Example (NIST 800-171)
The analysis process starts with broad categories and drills down to the resolution of small segments. The instructor demonstrates this using the NIST 800-171 Rev 2 document.
*The Significance:* By looking at the broad areas and breaking them down into individual security tasks, one can see how well the organization handles procedures and processes for each and every step.

## 5. The Final Report and the Way Forward
After gathering information on all processes and devices across all locations, a final document summarizing the findings must be created.
*Report Components:*
1. *Comparison:* Where we are today versus the detailed baseline objectives.
2. *The Pathway:* This is the more complex part – how to get from the current state to the desired state.
    ◦ This path usually requires: time, money, purchasing equipment, and executing a Change Control process to implement the changes in the environment.
3. *Recommendations:* All recommendations for meeting the baseline are documented in the gap analysis report.

## 6. Visualization and Prioritization
To make the report useful, it is customary to use tables and colors (Heatmaps) that illustrate the gaps across different locations of the organization.
*Example of an Analysis Table:* Suppose the organization has 7 different locations (Remote Sites), and we are checking a series of system requirements in each of them.
• *Green:* Locations that are relatively close to meeting the baseline.
• *Yellow:* Locations that are at the midpoint.
• *Red:* Locations that require a lot of work to meet the standards.
*Action Strategy:* If we want to achieve the biggest impact on improving security:
1. We will start with the requirements and locations marked in Red.
2. After that, we will move to those marked in Yellow.
3. Finally, we will address the Green ones.
The report will include an extensive explanation of why these colors were chosen and a summary of how to implement the security controls to meet the objectives.

## Executive Summary
Gap Analysis is a strategic and complex process for comparing the organization's current security posture against a desired Baseline (such as NIST SP 800-171 or ISO/IEC 27001). The process includes an in-depth analysis of people, processes, and systems, while breaking down broad requirements (such as Access Control) into specific tasks (such as Provisioning). The outcome of the process is a report that details not only the weaknesses but also the resources (time, budget, equipment) required to fix them. It is customary to prioritize treating the most severe gaps (for example, areas marked in red) to maximize the improvement of the security posture.
