# Lesson: Privacy
**Module:** 5.4

## The Scope of Privacy Laws
Organizations collect a staggering, unprecedented amount of data regarding individuals. Consequently, immense, legally binding international frameworks exist strictly dictating how an organization must securely handle this sensitive information.

*   **Local / National Laws:** Privacy legislation heavily depends on the jurisdiction. The United States utilizes sector-specific laws (e.g., HIPAA for healthcare).
*   **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation):** A massive, extraordinarily strict regulation heavily enforcing data privacy for every citizen residing within the European Union (EU), regardless of where in the world the company processing the data is physically located.

## The Rights of the Data Subject
Privacy laws, particularly the GDPR, fundamentally shift the immense power of data ownership away from the massive corporation and return it aggressively to the individual user (the **Data Subject**).
*   **The Data Subject:** Any individual whose personal data (name, genetics, location, social media posts) is being collected.
*   **The Right to be Forgotten:** A monumental GDPR provision. A Data Subject possesses the absolute legal authority to demand an organization immediately, permanently, and violently delete all private data regarding that individual from their sprawling corporate databases.

## Data Hierarchy and Responsibilities
As defined by privacy frameworks, organizations maintain strictly defined roles governing data:
*   **Data Owner:** Typically a high-level executive (e.g., VP of Sales) who holds the ultimate, foundational legal liability and broad responsibility for a specific dataset.
*   **Data Controller:** The entity that ultimately dictates exactly *why* data is collected and precisely *how* it will be utilized. (e.g., The internal HR department decides bank info is needed for payroll).
*   **Data Processor:** The entity (often an external third-party) that executes the actual, physical processing of the data, acting strictly and legally exclusively upon the unyielding commands of the Data Controller.

## Data Inventory and Retention
An organization cannot mathematically secure data, nor comply with massive privacy regulations, if it does not explicitly know what data it intrinsically possesses.
*   **Data Inventory:** A meticulous, comprehensive, centrally managed database explicitly cataloging every single piece of data stored by the company. It intensely documents who truly owns the data, its specific format, and precisely how frequently it is updated.
*   **Data Retention:** Organizations cannot legally retain data indefinitely. Privacy laws often aggressively mandate data retention policies detailing exactly how long specific data must legally be preserved, and precisely when it must be permanently, cryptographically decimated.
*   **Third-Party Transfer:** Before an organization ever shares data with an external vendor, the Data Controller must rigorously verify against the colossal Data Inventory that the transfer strictly adheres to all massive international privacy regulations (often utilizing Non-Disclosure Agreements to technically enforce privacy).
