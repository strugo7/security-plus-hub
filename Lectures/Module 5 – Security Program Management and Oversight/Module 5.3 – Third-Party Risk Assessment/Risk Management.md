Risk Management: A Guide to Agreements and Contracts with Third Parties

The lesson focuses on the importance of contracts and agreements as part of risk management when working with vendors and third parties 1.

Below are the main types of agreements covered:

1. Service and Performance Agreements
SLA (Service Level Agreement): This document defines the minimum conditions for a service provided by a third party (e.g., an internet provider). It includes requirements for system availability (Uptime), maximum allowed downtime, and commitments for handling faults (such as sending a technician or holding replacement equipment) 1, 2.
MSA (Master Service Agreement): This is a legal contract that establishes a framework for an ongoing relationship between two organizations. It defines basic terms like payment and billing arrangements and serves as an "umbrella" for future work, eliminating the need to renegotiate the terms for every new project 3, 4.
SOW (Statement of Work): This document usually accompanies the MSA and specifies a particular body of work. It includes the scope of work, location, timelines, and expected deliverables. It is a critical document that allows one to check whether all requirements have been met upon completion of the work 4.
2. Initial Cooperation Agreements
MOU (Memorandum of Understanding): A non-formal document (not a signed contract) that defines broad objectives for future cooperation between two organizations. It sometimes includes initial confidentiality statements 2.
MOA (Memorandum of Agreement): One step above the MOU. It details the relationship between the parties more and may include legally binding information, but is still not considered a full, enforceable contract by itself 3.
3. Confidentiality and Partnership Agreements
NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement): A formal contract that allows parties to share trade secrets and sensitive information without fear of disclosure. The agreement can be unilateral (only one party keeps the secret), bilateral (mutual), or multilateral 5.
BPA (Business Partners Agreement): Designed to create a formal partnership and defines the financial details, ownership percentages, and who is authorized to make business decisions. It also details scenarios for handling crises, such as financial problems or disasters that shut down the business 6.
