# Lesson: Agreement Types
**Module:** 5.3

## Introduction: Risk Management: Guide to Agreements and Contracts with Third Parties
The lesson focuses on the importance of contracts and agreements as part of risk management when working with vendors and third parties.
Here are the main types of agreements learned:

## 1. Service and Execution Agreements
• *SLA (Service Level Agreement):* This document defines the minimum conditions for the service provided by a third party (for example, an internet service provider). It includes requirements for system availability (Uptime), maximum allowable downtime, and commitments for handling faults (such as dispatching a technician or maintaining replacement equipment).
• *MSA (Master Service Agreement):* A legal contract that establishes a framework for an ongoing relationship between two organizations. It defines basic terms like payment and billing arrangements, and serves as a foundation ("umbrella") for future work, so that terms do not need to be renegotiated in every new project.
• *SOW (Statement of Work):* This document usually accompanies an MSA and details a specific job. It includes the scope of the work (Scope), the location, schedules, and expected deliverables (Deliverables). This is a critical document that makes it possible to verify at the end of the work if all requirements were fulfilled.

## 2. Initial Cooperation Agreements
• *MOU (Memorandum of Understanding):* An informal document (not a signed contract) that defines broad goals for future cooperation between two organizations. It sometimes includes initial confidentiality statements.
• *MOA (Memorandum of Agreement):* One step above the MOU. It details the relationship between the parties more comprehensively and may include legally binding information, but it is still not considered a full and enforceable contract on its own.

## 3. Confidentiality and Partnership Agreements
• *NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement):* A formal contract allowing parties to share trade secrets and sensitive information without fear of its exposure. The agreement can be unilateral (only one party maintains confidentiality), bilateral (mutual), or multilateral.
• *BPA (Business Partners Agreement):* Designed for creating a formal partnership and defines the financial details, ownership percentages, and who is authorized to make business decisions. Additionally, it details scenarios for handling crises, such as financial issues or disasters that shut down the business.
