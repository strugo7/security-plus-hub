# Lesson: Audits and Assessments

## Introduction: Changing the Perception of Audits
The term "Audit" often has a negative connotation. People tend to fear auditors looking for mistakes. However, in the context of cybersecurity (IT Security), there are excellent reasons to perform routine audits. A cybersecurity audit allows us to proactively examine many aspects of our computing environment, with the goal of improving overall defense.

## 1. Audit Objectives and Components
An audit is not a superficial check, but an in-depth examination of the organization's components. What do we check during an audit? According to the study material, an audit allows us to examine the following components:
1. *IT Infrastructure:* The servers, storage, and network.
2. *Software:* The applications and systems used by the organization.
3. *Communicating Devices:* All the devices that transmit and receive data on the network.
4. *Policies and Procedures:* Checking if our existing procedures actually provide adequate protection against today's modern threats.
*The Practical Context and Value to the Organization:* The ultimate value of an audit is the ability to find vulnerabilities in the network before hackers find them. In doing so, the audit effectively makes the organization safer. Instead of reacting to an incident, we prevent it.

## 2. The Concept of "Attestation"
A critical concept for the exam and professional work is Attestation. Often you will hear the terms "Audit" and "Attestation" in the same breath.
• *Definition:* Attestation is an "Opinion of truth" associated with the audit results.
• *The Process:* We perform the audit (the technical action/check), and then we provide an attestation to these results. This is essentially the official confirmation that the audit results faithfully reflect the situation on the ground.

## 3. Types of Audits: Internal vs. External
It is customary to divide the world of audits into two main categories, depending on who performs them and the reason they are performed.
**A. Internal Audit**
It is not always necessary to bring in an external party. Organizations can and should perform audits themselves.
• *Purpose of the Internal Audit:* To answer questions regarding Compliance. The organization checks itself to ensure all compliance tasks are performed properly and organizational procedures are followed.
• *Audit Committee:* In most organizations, there is a group called the "Audit Committee".
    ◦ *Role:* This group is responsible for Risk Management in the organization.
    ◦ *Authority:* The committee is the body that initiates (Starting) and concludes (Stopping) the internal audits.
• *Self-Assessment:* Internal audits usually start with a self-assessment process. The organization examines its internal processes and procedures and sees how well they align with the requirements. The audit committee compiles all these self-assessments to get a snapshot of where the organization stands relative to compliance requirements.

## Executive Summary
Audits are an essential managerial and security tool enabling an organization to identify vulnerabilities before they are exploited by attackers. The audit process concludes with an Attestation (verifying the truth of the results). The audit can be performed internally (led by the audit committee and via self-assessment) or externally (by a third party, usually as a regulatory requirement). The overarching goal is twofold: ensuring Compliance and improving the organization's resilience against threats.
