# Lesson: Security Procedures
**Module:** 5.1

## Operating Security
While Policies provide the "why" and Standards provide the "what," **Procedures** define the explicit, step-by-step "how." They are the deeply granular, operational instructions that technicians blindly follow to successfully execute complex security tasks without causing catastrophic errors.

## The Change Management Procedure
As discussed in security policies, Change Management is paramount. The *procedure* dictates the rigorous, mechanical steps required to implement the change:
1.  **Determine Scope:** Precisely identify the single application or the 500 endpoints profoundly affected by the structural alteration.
2.  **Analyze Risk:** Mathematically calculate the potential downtime and severity of the impact if the firewall update catastrophically fails.
3.  **Create the Backup Plan:** Document the exact command-line syntax required to instantly rollback the router configuration to its pristine, pre-change state.
4.  **Change Control Board (CCB):** Submit the massive documentation packet to the CCB for rigorous peer review, formal approval, and precise scheduling.

## Onboarding and Offboarding Procedures
The lifecycle of an employee represents massive risk. The procedures must be flawless.
*   **Onboarding:** The procedure dictates an unyielding checklist: The new hire physically signs the heavily legally binding Acceptable Use Policy (AUP). The Help Desk provisions the Active Directory account, rigidly applies the correct Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) departmental permissions, and issues the fully encrypted corporate laptop.
*   **Offboarding:** When an employee leaves, the procedure acts as a rapid, violent containment mechanism. The procedure dictates that the immediate first action is fundamentally disabling (not deleting) the Active Directory account, instantly terminating VPN access, recovering the decryption keys, and finally executing the physical reclamation of the corporate laptop.

## Playbooks and Runbooks
In the terrifying midst of a massive security incident, humans panic. Consequently, organizations create **Playbooks**.
*   **The Function:** A playbook is a meticulous, step-by-step instructional manual explicitly dictating the exact technical response to specific, catastrophic events.
*   **The Scenarios:** There is a specific playbook for reacting to a systemic Ransomware infection across the domain, and an entirely different, distinct playbook for investigating the theft of a highly privileged Administrator credential. The playbook tells the panicked engineer exactly what to quarantine first, what logs to aggressively pull second, and who to boldly notify third.

## SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response)
Modern environments utilize SOAR platforms to heavily automate these complex procedures.
*   **The Integration:** SOAR integrates hyper-diverse products into a singular command console. 
*   **The Automation:** Instead of a human manually reading the "Malware Playbook" and typing commands, the SOAR platform detects the malware, autonomously executes the playbook, communicates instantly with the firewall API to globally block the IP, and commands the Endpoint Detection (EDR) agent to violently kill the malicious process on the laptop in milliseconds without human intervention.

## Governance Structure
Procedures and policies are ultimately directed by a formal Governance structure.
*   **The Board:** A high-level Board of Directors or steering committee defines the massive, overarching strategic security objectives.
*   **The Committee:** A dedicated committee of Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) aggressively develops the granular policies and precise procedures required to successfully implement those massive board-level objectives.
*   **Public vs. Private:** Governance in a private corporation operates with profound secrecy. Conversely, Governance in the Public Sector (Government) operates under massive legal mandates requiring intense public transparency and open documentation.
*   **Centralized vs. Decentralized:** Governance can act as one massive, centralized authority dictating rules globally, or act as heavily decentralized regional units possessing localized authority.
