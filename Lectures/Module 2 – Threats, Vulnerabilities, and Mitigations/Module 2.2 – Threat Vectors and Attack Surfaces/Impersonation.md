# Lesson: Impersonation
**Module:** 2.2

## What is Impersonation?
**Impersonation** is when an attacker pretends to be someone that they are not. This is a common social engineering tactic where an actor invents a story (a pretext) to elicit trust from their target.

**Examples of Impersonation Attempts:**
*   *"Hello sir. My name is Wendy, and I'm from Microsoft Windows. This is an urgent checkup call..."*
*   *"This is an enforcement action executed by the US Treasury intending your serious attention."*
*   *"Congratulations on your excellent payment history. You now qualify for 0% interest rates..."*

These are all examples of attackers impersonating technical support, government entities, and financial institutions.

## Establishing Trust and Authority
Impersonation is fundamentally used to make the target more trustful and comfortable with providing sensitive data.
*   **Internal Trust** An attacker might call and say they are from your company's help desk to resolve an issue on your system. Thinking they work for the same company immediately provides a level of trust.
*   **Rank and Authority** An attacker may introduce themselves as the Vice President of Finance or an executive. By pretending to be someone higher in rank, they hope you will supply the requested information without questioning it.
*   **Technical Jargon** Attackers often use sophisticated technical terms to focus your thinking on the details of what they are saying, rather than whether the call itself is legitimate.

## Eliciting Information (Vishing)
The ultimate goal of impersonation is to elicit information. This is very commonly seen with voice phishing (**vishing**), where someone calls with a story that eventually prompts you to give up your credit card number, Social Security number, or bank details. 

Because an attacker can't just ask "what's your bank account number?", they rely on their pretext. They might claim your account number is needed because there's been a problem sending a payment, leveraging the persona to gain your trust throughout the story.

## Identity Fraud
Attackers also use impersonation to take advantage of **identity fraud**—pretending to be you using your private information.
*   **Fraudulent Credit** They might open a new credit card account using your information but route the mailing address to themselves. They purchase products without intending to pay, ruining your credit report.
*   **Fraudulent Bank Accounts** By opening checking accounts in your name, attackers can store funds from illegal activities, or even take out loans under your name and disappear with the cash.
*   **Government Benefits** A common form of identity fraud is tax fraud, where the attacker uses your details to file tax returns on your behalf and routes your overpayment refund to their own account.

## How to Prevent Impersonation
There are a number of essential steps you can take to prevent impersonation attacks within your organization:
1.  **Do Not Volunteer Information** If someone calls from the "help desk" and asks for your password, it is a phishing attempt. Legitimate support teams do not need your password to gain access to your system.
2.  **Verify Their Identity** You should never give away your personal information over the telephone to an unverified caller. Ask if you can verify them, hang up, and call a public, officially listed phone number to ensure you are speaking to the correct organization.
3.  **Implement Verification Procedures** Relying on immediate verification should be a standard, required process within any organization. No one should take a phone call at face value if it involves sensitive financial information or critical details.
