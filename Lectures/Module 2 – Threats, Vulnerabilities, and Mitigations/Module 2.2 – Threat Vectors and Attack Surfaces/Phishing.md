# Lesson: Phishing
**Module:** 2.2

## Introduction to Phishing
**Phishing** is a term we use to describe social engineering that uses various communications methods to make you think something is real when it isn't. Phishing is usually delivered by mail, text message, or other communication methods to trick you into giving up information that normally would be private (such as a username and password).

The attackers are trying to convince you that they are a legitimate organization or someone you trust, often misdirecting you to click links that are inside of an email.

## Identifying Phishing
It's important to be able to identify a phishing attempt before interacting with it. Several key indicators can give a phishing email away:
*   **Incorrect Links** The links in the message might not point towards a well-known, trusted site. They often go to completely different, suspicious locations.
*   **Visual Irregularities** There are typically problems with spacing, fonts, and bad grammar that wouldn't normally appear in professional corporate communications.
*   **Urgency and Deadlines** Phishing messages frequently try to create a sense of urgency (e.g., "we notice your email has not been confirmed for the new upgraded service"). They establish arbitrary deadlines to pressure you into acting immediately before you have a chance to think critically.
*   **Suspicious Sender Address** An email that pretends to be a corporate service might originate from an obvious personal or non-associated domain, such as an `icloud.com` address pretending to be a Rackspace or Apple support address.

## Techniques Used by Attackers
*   **Spoofed Email Addresses** Attackers will either fully spoof the sender's address or use an address that looks very close to the legitimate company's email domain.
*   **Typosquatting** The attacker relies on misdirection, for example, using `professormessor.com` (spelled with an O instead of an E). If you don't look closely, you might assume you are visiting the legitimate `professormesser.com` domain.
*   **Pretexting** This is when attackers formulate a story or scenario (a pretext) to drag you into a drama, in hopes that you'll drop your guard and provide your credentials.

## Specific Types of Phishing
Phishing can occur over many different mediums beyond standard email:
*   **Vishing (Voice Phishing)** When an attacker calls you directly on the phone. They might spoof their caller ID to say they are from "Visa" or your local bank, inventing a story about a failed payment to extract your credit card information or account details.
*   **Smishing (SMS Phishing)** If you have a mobile phone, you have likely received phishing over text messages. You might receive a text pretending to be the USPS claiming a package was suspended due to an incorrect delivery address and providing a malicious link to steal your login. 
*   **Other Scams** Fraudsters consistently invent new methodologies, including fake check scams, phone verification code scams, and more. Being familiar with these vectors is the best way to stop yourself and your organization from falling victim.

## The Consequences of Falling Victim
If an attacker manages to get your username and password, they can:
*   Send malicious emails from your trusted account to infiltrate other users in your organization.
*   Search your existing emails for financial intelligence or login portals to other services.
*   Access external financial services (such as PayPal) and use the password reset feature since they control the recovery email.
*   Have you download malware or ransomware directly onto your system by clicking the phishing link.
