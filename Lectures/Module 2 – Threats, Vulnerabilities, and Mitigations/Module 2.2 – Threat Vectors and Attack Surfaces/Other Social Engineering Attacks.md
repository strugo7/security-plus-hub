# Lesson: Other Social Engineering Attacks

## Introduction
Attackers use many techniques to gain access to our systems. Aside from phishing and impersonation, there are highly targeted campaigns built around deception. In this video, you’ll learn about misinformation campaigns and brand impersonation.

## Misinformation Campaigns
One very effective social engineering technique is to disseminate incorrect information to others. Unlike a simple difference of opinion, **misinformation** and **disinformation** contain factually incorrect details. It is usually expressly designed to divide or confuse different groups of people. 
*   **Influence Campaigns:** Often found on social media sites, these campaigns center around political or social issues.
*   **Nation State Operations:** There are documented cases where a third-party government or nation state uses these campaigns to create dissension. It can persuade people to believe falsehoods or distract from damaging truths.
*   **Delivery Mechanism:** These messages can be delivered through advertising or organic social media posts.

### The Lifecycle of Misinformation
1.  **Creating Fake Users:** The attacker creates multiple accounts belonging to fake users (bots or personas).
2.  **Generating Content:** The attacker uses one of their fake accounts to post the engineered content on a social network.
3.  **Amplification:** The attacker leverages their network of fake accounts to 'like, share, or follow' the content, artificially boosting its popularity.
4.  **Algorithmic Boost:** The social media algorithm recognizes the activity and presents the post to actual legitimate users.
5.  **Mainstream Recognition:** Once it reaches a certain level of popularity, mass media may report on it, giving credibility to manufactured misinformation.

## Brand Impersonation
Another social engineering technique relies on impersonating highly recognizable brand names (e.g., Coca-Cola, McDonald’s).

*   **Execution:** Attackers create hundreds or thousands of fraudulent sites using the brand name. When search engines like Google index these sites, unsuspecting searchers are redirected to an impersonated site.
*   **The Trap:** Once on the site, users may encounter pop-up messages claiming they won a prize, offering a special deal, or advising them to download a "required" piece of software.
*   **The Impact:** The downloaded software contains malware. The user's system becomes infected, resulting in unwanted ads, web tracking, or the unauthorized exfiltration of private data.
