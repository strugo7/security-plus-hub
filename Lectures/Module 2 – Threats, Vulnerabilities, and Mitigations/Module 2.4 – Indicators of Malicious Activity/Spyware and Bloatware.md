# Lesson: Spyware and Bloatware
**Module:** 2.4

## Spyware
If you ever feel like your computer is secretly watching everything you do, you might be infected with **spyware**. Spyware is a specific category of malware explicitly designed to track, monitor, and report your activities back to an attacker's servers.

### Characteristics of Spyware
*   **Infection Vectors:** Spyware infects a system utilizing the same vectors as traditional viruses—bundled into peer-to-peer software, masquerading as fake security software (scareware), or through malicious email links.
*   **Monitoring Activity:** It quietly monitors your web browsing habits to push highly targeted, forced advertising to your screen, generating affiliate revenue for the attacker.
*   **Keylogging:** Advanced spyware frequently incorporates keylogger functionalities, secretly recording every username, password, credit card number, and message typed into the keyboard.
*   **Evasion and Persistence:** Spyware developers write highly resilient code that embeds deeply into the operating system architecture, making entirely manual removal exceptionally difficult.

### Defensive Strategies Against Spyware
*   **Anti-Malware Tools:** Third-party anti-malware scanners (e.g., Malwarebytes) specialize in identifying and cleanly extracting deeply embedded spyware from the OS.
*   **Installation Discipline:** Only install software from highly reputable, verified sources to minimize the risk of inadvertently approving a spyware bundled payload.

## Bloatware
**Bloatware** refers to legitimate but entirely unnecessary software applications pre-installed on new computers or mobile devices by the hardware manufacturer. While not inherently malicious like spyware, bloatware severely degrades the default user experience and system security.

### Characteristics and Risks of Bloatware
*   **The Financial Motive:** Third-party software companies pay hardware manufacturers to bundle their trial utilities, games, and security scanners into the base OS image, guaranteeing immediate massive distribution.
*   **Performance Degradation:** Many of these pre-installed applications configure themselves to automatically boot in the background at startup, silently consuming valuable CPU, RAM, and storage allocation unnecessarily.
*   **Security Vulnerabilities:** Every application installed on a device expands its attack surface. Pre-installed bloatware may contain severe, unpatched vulnerabilities out of the box, offering threat actors an immediate exploit path on a brand-new device.

### Remediating Bloatware
*   **Standard Uninstallation:** Users should manually review the operating system's installed programs list and uninstall unrecognized or unnecessary applications.
*   **Third-Party Uninstallers:** Some uncooperative bloatware hides its uninstaller natively. In these instances, users may deploy specialized third-party uninstaller utilities to forcibly strip the software from the system registry.
