# Lesson: Threat Actors
**Module:** 2.1

## Introduction to Threat Actors
A **threat actor** is an entity that is the cause of an event that affects the security of others. We often refer to these actors as malicious actors because the actions that they take tend to have a negative effect on the security of others. When you refer to an attack, it’s useful to know who the threat actor is to get a better understanding of why this attack is happening and what their ultimate goal was. 

Attackers can come from anywhere:
*   **Internal Threat** The attackers work for your organization and are inside the company itself.
*   **External Threat** They may be outside the organization, trying to gain access through public resources.

The number of available resources, financial funding, and level of sophistication are ways to characterize the threat actor. 

## Motivations
There are many different reasons a threat actor might decide to launch an attack. These can include a need to find data to exfiltrate, espionage by a competitor, disrupting services to create problems for customers, or a variety of philosophical and political reasons depending on the situation, the attacker, and the target.

## Nation States
Usually on the outside of your organization, a **nation state** represents an entire government or an arm of that government dealing with national security.
*   **Motivations** Data exfiltration, philosophical or political reasons, service disruption, or potentially pulling a target into a war.
*   **Resources and Sophistication** A government has enormous resources available. They can afford to have the most sophisticated developers creating very advanced attack types and can attack multiple locations at the same time.
*   **Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs)** Nation state attacks are often referred to as APTs because of their high capability and continuous presence. They target military control locations, utilities, or a country's finances. (e.g., the Stuxnet worm, created by the US and Israel to attack nuclear centrifuges).

## Unskilled Attackers
These are attackers with very low technical knowledge who may run scripts without any knowledge of what's happening under the surface.
*   **Capabilities** If a script works, the attack is successful. If it doesn't, they do not have the skills to modify the exploit.
*   **Motivations** Driven by the attack itself, disrupting services, exfiltration, or philosophical/political reasons.
*   **Resources** They generally don't have a lot of resources available and rely on the easiest ways in, using readily available scripts (often referred to as script kiddies).

## Hacktivists
A hacker motivated by political reasons, a philosophical difference, or a desire to disrupt an organization is known as a **hacktivist** (hacker activist).
*   **Capabilities** Often very sophisticated technologists who attack in specific ways, such as denial of service (DoS), defacing websites, or finding and leaking private documents.
*   **Resources** They don't typically have a large amount of personal finances available, but some organizations fundraise to support their hacktivism campaigns.

## Insider Threats
An **insider threat** is an especially difficult problem to locate and stop. This goes beyond poor security hygiene (like sticky-note passwords) and represents a malicious actor inside the network.
*   **Motivations** Often out for revenge or financial gain against the organization.
*   **Capabilities** The attacker takes advantage of all resources that already exist within the network. They have a "medium" level of sophistication, but they excel in knowing exactly where the data is and how to circumvent existing controls. Proper vetting during hiring is critical to preventing insider threats.

## Organized Crime
In cybersecurity, **organized crime** is highly prevalent.
*   **Motivations** Highly motivated by financial gain and profit.
*   **Structure and Resources** Since they are seeking profit, they have substantial resources. They use a corporate structure where different teams specialize in hacking, managing exploits, selling data to third parties, or even providing customer support (frequently seen in ransomware operations).

## Shadow IT
A threat actor that is internal to your organization but not inherently malicious is **Shadow IT**. This is usually a group or department that works around the rules established by the IT department.
*   **Operations** They build their own infrastructure, install applications, and use cloud-based services with their own budget or credit cards, entirely bypassing IT change control or security protocols.
*   **Risks** Since they often lack an IT or security background, they do not implement proper backups, change control, or basic security policies, placing the entire organization at extreme risk of a breach.
